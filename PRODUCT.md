# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Future OCR initially serves developers and small businesses that need reliable printed-text extraction without operating OCR infrastructure. Subscribers submit Arabic, English, or bilingual images and scanned PDFs through a focused web workspace or authenticated API. Internal operators use a separate control panel to manage tenants, plans, jobs, usage, and system health.

## Product Purpose

Future OCR converts bounded, untrusted documents into traceable machine-readable text. Success means a subscriber can create an account, submit a document, monitor processing, review or download the result, understand usage, and manage API access while operators can safely diagnose the service without viewing document content by default.

## Brand Personality

Professional, calm, trustworthy. The interface should communicate careful handling, predictable processing, and technical competence without feeling clinical or intimidating.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-tool marketing with purple gradients, glowing effects, or exaggerated claims.
- Dense hosting panels that expose implementation details before users need them.
- Consumer upload tools with unclear retention, privacy, or processing status.
- Decorative dashboards filled with low-value metric cards.

## Design Principles

1. Make document state unmistakable from upload through deletion.
2. Put privacy, retention, and usage facts beside the decisions they affect.
3. Use familiar controls and progressive disclosure so the workflow remains fast.
4. Treat Arabic and English as equal product languages, not translated afterthoughts.
5. Give operators evidence and safe actions without exposing subscriber content.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard-only operation, visible focus, reduced motion, sufficient contrast, semantic status announcements, and layouts that remain usable at 200% zoom. Arabic uses native right-to-left layout and mixed-direction content handling from the first release.
